## Clock and reset profile

Use a 7.502 ns local DFI clock with equal high and low portions. If testing the USER link, use a separately phased 8 ns USER clock with 2 ns high time. For DFI reset use `reset_release_sync_data` with `DATA_RESET_COPIES=0, FALLING_RELEASE=1`. Drive its common cold reset low asynchronously, including while DFI is stopped. Its data reset output deasserts just after rising edge R3 and its state reset output just after F3, the falling edge between R3 and R4. R1 is the first rising DFI edge after cold reset deassertion. Neither release advances with DFI stopped. A new assertion, including between R3 and F3, cancels the pending release without waiting for an edge.

Use `SEPARATE_DATA_RESET=1` on `dol2_axi_write_arbiter` and `raw_video_record_formatter`. Connect their state reset inputs `i_axi_rst_n` and `i_rst_n` to `o_rst_n`, and their `i_data_reset_n` to `o_data_rst_n`. The first legal active DFI rising sample is R4. VALID and READY mean a transfer only on a rising edge. Before R4 an idle `o_start_ready`, `o_drained` or `o_quiescent` may become visible after R3. There can be no AW, W, B or payload transfer prior to R4. With `SEPARATE_DATA_RESET=0`, the added input is ignored and idle levels are qualified by the state reset. Verify rising-edge transfers and meaningful data; the idle levels during R3-to-F3 can differ.

## AXI complete-burst arbitration

The production DFI routing is a two-level tree. Two writers in each eye share an inner instance. Two eyes can share an outer instance. A short structural carrier can use one inner instance connected as client 0 of the outer instance, and tie unused outer client 1 off. Connect public AW/W/B ports end to end. Bit zero/low slices belong to client 0. On the active clock edge, the arbiter captures the address of a pending client, presents AW, and accepts W only after AW completes. An accepted AW owns the port through all `AWLEN+1` W beats and the B handshake. The owner receives only its B, with actual BID/BRESP. VALID and payload for offered AW, W and B stay stable during a finite READY stall. WLAST must occur on the last beat. Legal writes use 128-bit payload, `AWSIZE=4`, `AWBURST=1`, 16-byte-aligned addresses and stay within a 4 KiB boundary.

Offer a legal AW as soon as F3 releases the state, hold through handshake, send its exact W beats, and wait for the same client to receive matching B with `BRESP=0`. With one active inner and one outer arbitration level and READY already high, the initial AW accepts at R6. Present two contending clients, exercise a finite AW/W or B stall, and check no second grant while a B is outstanding. For equal contenders after reset client 0 wins first. After each complete transaction the other pending client has priority. Quiescent means no committed AW..B or offered address, data or response; a held B is still owned. No fault is legal on these transactions.

For the interrupted case, stop DFI with a downstream AW held under stall before acceptance. Assert cold reset, check that both levels withdraw AWVALID and all W/B transfers, and clear the upstream offer during reset. Release while stopped, check that there is no new offer without an edge, then allow R1–F3. A fresh distinct AW/W/B must finish with no old data or response. Keep the external memory BFM in the same cold cohort during this test.

## ARAW formatter and returned SAFE

Use an 8×2 image, RAW10 (16 pixels, 20 payload bytes), one nonzero session, fixed epoch, distinct sequences, camera 0 or 1, and a nonzero job ID. Hold the descriptor from before `i_start_valid` through complete emission. Stall `m_ready` for the first valid header and check stable meaningful output. START is `i_start_valid && o_start_ready` at a rising edge. If offered before the cold startup, the first common legal acceptance edge is R4. Header is four 128-bit words, each KEEP `ffff`, no LAST. Its 64 little-endian bytes are defined by the checked-in ARAW JSON. Low byte of each data word is first. Payload follows unmodified. Offer first KEEP `ffff`, LAST=0, then KEEP `000f`, LAST=1. Hold upstream payload through `s_valid && s_ready`. `o_done` is true on the accepted final payload beat for a successful record. `o_drained` returns true after that edge. Reset during a held header or payload withdraws the offer without a clock. After restart, no pre-reset record or bytes may transfer and a second fresh record must complete.

Run a short end-to-end case with the real `product_record_cdc` and its production FIFO. Use the default `MAX_RECORD_BYTES=16779328`. The source cold reset is common with the formatter, and USER can use the same cold assertion. Wait for both public `s_reset_done` and `m_reset_done`. Couple the START and record handshakes as in production: formatter `i_start_valid=offer && s_record_ready`, CDC `s_record_valid=offer && o_start_ready`. Connect formatter output data/KEEP/VALID/LAST to the CDC source AXIS and connect READY back. Provide `s_record_length=84` and a distinctive 128-bit identity, held until returned source retirement. On the source side latch the final `o_done` transfer as `s_done_valid && s_done_ok`; supply `s_drained` when done and `o_drained` are both true. Hold the two success levels while the transaction is owned. USER accepts the held record descriptor, stalls its AXIS briefly, then consumes exactly 84 bytes, with matching header and payload, followed by `m_done_valid && m_done_ok && m_drained`. Send matching USER `m_retire_identity`, a distinctive `m_retire_status`, and `m_retire_valid` only after the success terminal. Wait for the actual USER SAFE handshake (`m_retire_valid && m_retire_ready`) and then a separate source retirement handshake (`s_retire_valid && s_retire_ready`) with the same identity and status. No new record can replace the outstanding identity before the source handshake. No transaction advances on an unaccepted offer.
